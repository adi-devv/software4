Language Models Need Sleep

A model that learns while it works, then goes offline to consolidate what it learned into slower, more stable memory, and to practise on its own "dreams".

Behrouz, Hashemi, Javanmard, Mirrokni Google Research + Cornell arXiv 2606.03979 · Jun 2026 (v2 Jul) Read the paper
The problem

Deployed models can't keep what they learn

Anything a model picks up in context disappears when the context ends. Fine-tuning or LoRA on new data makes it forget old skills. Re-pretraining costs too much. The paper copies the brain's answer: learn fast while awake, then move the lessons into stable memory during sleep.

Consolidation: what trains, what stays frozen

Memory is a chain of blocks that update at different speeds, from fast to stable, which the authors call a Continuum Memory System. Consolidation moves knowledge one step down the chain without disturbing what's already there.

Step 1 · expand

Add a new low-rank expert

new params

A small, empty mixture-of-experts slot is added to the next, more stable block.

Step 2 · seed

Distil into the new expert only

new expert trainseverything else frozen

The old model is the teacher. It generates "dreams" and the new expert learns to match it, through distillation plus an RL imitation reward.

Step 3 · update

Commit and prune

expert activatedfast block reset

Apply the update, turn on the new expert, and clear the fast block's old experts. The paper compares this to synaptic pruning.

The imitation reward is a 0/1 semantic match judged by a frozen reward model, plus string similarity to the teacher's continuation.

Caveats

Read it with care

  • All numbers are the authors' own. No independent replication yet.
  • There is no limitations section. Tests are at 1–8B scale and mostly on Google's own Hope architecture, not a stock LLM with an adapter.
  • A different CMU/UMD paper (2605.26099, "Do Language Models Need Sleep?") is only about offline recurrence over context. It doesn't do lifelong learning.
